## Whites
- **Lead white** — basic lead carbonate; the dominant Western white for ~2,000 years (opaque, warm, flexible). Doubled as toxic cosmetic ("ceruse"); Countess of Coventry died of it (1760). Replaced by zinc/titanium for toxicity.
- **Ivory** — walrus/elephant tusk. Queen Victoria's 1840 ivory wedding satin set the Western bridal default (*a Western convention, not universal*). Trade decimated elephants; mammoth tusk now a legal alternative.
- **Silver** — moon-associated (vs. gold/sun) in classical/medieval Europe; funded the Spanish Empire via Potosí; "silver tongue/lining" link to eloquence and hope.
- **Whitewash** — slaked lime; cheap, antibacterial (plague Hong Kong 1894); used to erase pre-Reformation church murals; Le Corbusier framed all-white walls as a moral cleansing (1925).
- **Isabelline** — dingy yellow-white; the "unwashed underwear" siege legend is a 19th-century invention (the name predates it); now a zoology term for pale buff animals.
- **Chalk** — calcium carbonate from marine fossils; Rembrandt bulked paint with it for impasto; the Uffington White Horse is chalk-filled trenches.
- **Beige** — French for undyed wool; "cosmic latte" is the averaged color of the universe (2002).

## Yellows
- **Blonde** — only ~2% of people are naturally blonde; Roman associations were foreign/sex-work; weaponized by Nazi racial pseudoscience; "dumb blonde" traces to an 18th-c. courtesan.
- **Lead-tin yellow** — dominant European yellow c.1300–1750 (Titian, Rembrandt, Vermeer); vanished ~1750, rediscovered/named only in 1941.
- **Indian yellow** — vivid mustard; made from the urine of cows fed only mango leaves; ceased almost immediately after being documented (1883), partly on animal-welfare grounds.
- **Naples yellow** — lead antimonate; one of the oldest synthetic pigments (Egypt); falsely thought volcanic (Dalí repeated it in 1948); blackens with iron tools.
- **Chrome yellow** — lead chromate; Van Gogh's *Sunflowers* yellow — and it is visibly browning as the pigment degrades in light (confirmed 2011).
- **Gamboge** — *Garcinia* tree sap (Cambodia); luminous; a violent purgative; used by Perrin to prove atoms exist (Brownian motion); discontinued 2005.
- **Orpiment** — arsenic trisulfide (~60% arsenic); gold-like sparkle; Caligula tried to smelt gold from it; highly toxic; reacts destructively with copper/lead pigments.
- **Imperial yellow** — reserved for the Chinese emperor from the Tang dynasty (AD 618); in the five-element system yellow = earth/center/dragon — the most cosmologically central color. *Specifically Chinese-imperial.*
- **Gold** — Klimt's gold-leaf "golden period"; Mansa Musa's 1324 hajj reportedly depressed regional gold prices for a decade; Field of the Cloth of Gold (1520).

## Oranges
- **Dutch orange** — the House of Orange; the Dutch bred orange carrots; the original Dutch flag's orange faded to red; New York's flag keeps an orange stripe.
- **Saffron** — *Crocus sativus* stigmas; ~$364/oz, tens of thousands of flowers per pound; a 1374 "Saffron War"; Buddhist robes are *not* actually saffron-dyed (too costly).
- **Amber** — fossil tree resin; Greek *elektron* (origin of "electron," from static electricity); the looted Amber Room never recovered.
- **Ginger** — reddish hair; English associations with fire/temper; Neanderthals may have had red hair; Pre-Raphaelite obsession (Siddal).
- **Minium** — lead tetroxide orange-red; root of "miniature" (via *miniator*, the manuscript red-painter); displaced by vermilion.
- **Nude** — historically assumed Caucasian skin; critiqued (Michelle Obama's "nude" gown, 2010); Louboutin later launched multiple nude tones (2013). *A loaded, non-neutral category — flag the assumption.*

## Pinks
- **Baker-Miller pink** — a specific bright pink claimed to weaken aggression; painted in a Seattle naval cell (1979); replication failed; rarely used now.
- **Mountbatten pink** — gray + Venetian red naval camouflage for dawn/dusk (1940); effectiveness debated; dropped 1942.
- **Puce** — French for "flea"; Louis XVI mocked Marie Antoinette's gown as "flea color" (1775) and the court promptly demanded puce.
- **Fuchsia** — blue-based pink named for botanist Leonhard Fuchs (who never saw the plant); voted one of Britain's least-liked colors (1998).
- **Shocking pink** — Schiaparelli's hot pink (1937); Monroe's *Gentlemen Prefer Blondes* dress referenced it.
- **Fluorescent pink** — re-emits UV as visible light; central to punk/rave visual language.
- **Amaranth** — Greek "unfading"; sacred Aztec food (blood-dough idols horrified Spanish priests, who banned it); EU dye E123, US-banned since 1976.

## Reds
- **Scarlet** — originally a fine *cloth*, not a color; kermes-insect dyed; cardinals' scarlet (1464) replaced lost Tyrian purple; Drebbel's 1606 tin-cochineal accident brightened it.
- **Cochineal** — *Dactylopius* insect on cactus; ~70,000 insects/lb; Spanish monopoly; still food coloring E120 (Starbucks dropped it 2012).
- **Vermilion** — mercuric sulfide (cinnabar); the bright 2,000-year-old Pompeii walls; at peak as costly as gold; Venice banned its toxic manufacture (1294). A warm, orange-leaning red. ≈ Munsell R5/9.
- **Rosso corsa** — Italian racing red (Borghese's 1907 win); Ferrari's signature; national racing colors (Italy red, Britain green, France blue, Germany silver).
- **Hematite** — iron oxide; one of the oldest pigments (250,000+ yrs); Egyptian "red ocher burials" worldwide; red = life/blood/sun in Egypt. *Burial symbolism is broad but not identical across cultures.*
- **Madder** — *Rubia* root → alizarin; "Turkey red" via a foul multi-stage process; synthetic alizarin (1868) collapsed the industry overnight.
- **Dragon's blood** — red tree resin; legend of dragon/elephant blood; faded badly — "does not merit the attention of the artist" (Field, 1835).

## Purples
- **Tyrian purple** — from ~250,000 murex snails per ounce; Phoenician Tyre; restricted to emperors by late Rome (death to wear it); "born in the purple" (Byzantine *Porphyra*); the craft died with Constantinople (1453). *Roman/Byzantine imperial — the canonical "exclusive" color.*
- **Archil** — lichen dye, red-to-purple; Welsh archil-dyed red cloaks reportedly spooked French invaders at Fishguard (1797).
- **Magenta** — aniline dye (1858) named for the Battle of Magenta (1859); a CMYK process color; early versions contained arsenic.
- **Mauve** — first synthetic aniline dye; Perkin, age 18, by accident (1856); royal adoption sparked "Mauve Measles"; later read as aging/conventional.
- **Heliotrope** — pale pink-purple; Victorian "half-mourning" color easing out of black.
- **Violet** — Impressionist "violettomania"; Manet: "the true color of the atmosphere... is violet" (violet = complement of sunlight-yellow, so shadows read violet). A perception/optics point, useful for shadow palettes.

## Blues
- **Ultramarine** — lapis lazuli (Afghanistan); historically costlier than gold, specified separately in artists' contracts; synthetic French ultramarine (1828) made it cheap; Yves Klein's IKB. A warm, red-biased blue.
- **Cobalt** — cobalt aluminate (1802); exposed Van Meegeren's fake "Vermeers" (the pigment postdated Vermeer by ~130 years). Neutral-cool; great grays with raw umber.
- **Indigo** — plant dye needing no mordant; "blue disease" in faded textiles; Tuareg "Blue Men"; synthetic indigo (1880s) underpins the denim industry. Very dark, can overpower mixes.
- **Prussian blue** — accidental Berlin discovery (~1705); ~1/10 the price of ultramarine but light-fades; Picasso's Blue Period; basis of cyanotype blueprints; treats thallium/cesium poisoning. Strong green-biased blue.
- **Egyptian blue** — calcium copper silicate; the first known synthetic pigment (~2500 BC, "artificial lapis"); manufacturing knowledge lost for ~1,900 years.
- **Woad** — *Isatis*; same indigo molecule at ~1/20 strength; Celtic body-paint accounts; not sumptuary-restricted, which helped blue overtake red in medieval Europe; anti-indigo "devil's color" laws protected woad.
- **Cerulean** — cobalt stannate; the UN chose it as "the opposite of red, the war color"; Hindu gods (Krishna, Shiva, Rama) shown blue for the infinite/divine. A cool, green-biased blue.

## Greens
- **Verdigris** — copper acetate/carbonate; van Eyck's Arnolfini green dress (since shifted); reacts with other pigments and attacks paper — "beautiful... but it does not last" (Cennini); the Statue of Liberty's patina.
- **Emerald (the gem-color)** — chromium-stained beryl; Shakespeare's "green-eyed monster" (jealousy); Nero's emerald "sunglasses."
- **Kelly green** — Irish identity; St. Patrick was originally associated with *blue*; green chosen to distinguish Catholics from Orange Order orange; the tricolor's green+orange+white = reconciliation. *Specifically Irish.*
- **Scheele's green** — copper arsenite; lethal arsenic wallpaper (≈100 sq mi in British homes by 1858); deaths documented; never formally banned in Britain. The "Napoleon poisoned by wallpaper" theory is unproven.
- **Terre verte** — "green earth," very stable but weak tinting; classic medieval underpaint for skin (cool green balances warm flesh); oddly absent from cave art.
- **Celadon** — gray-green ceramic glaze; named for a green-coated novel shepherd; Song China / Goryeo Korea; thought a poison-antidote by Turkish rulers; the legendary royal *mi se* turned out "a rather drab olive."
- **Avocado** — the 1970s "back to nature" decade color; tied to early environmentalism (Earth Day 1970, "Blue Marble" 1972).

## Browns
- **Khaki** — Urdu "dusty"; mud-dyed cotton (Peshawar, 1846); the first uniform designed to *blend in* rather than stand out; universal British WWI dress.
- **Buff** — pale tan ox-leather; "in the buff" = naked; Washington's buff-and-blue uniform (1775) became an American-independence symbol (adopted by British Whigs).
- **Sepia** — cuttlefish ink (melanin); high tinting strength, durable; used by Cicero and Leonardo; photographic sepia toning = nostalgia.
- **Umber** — manganese-bearing iron oxide; cool, stable, cheap, ideal for shadows; used at Lascaux (rediscovered 1940 by a dog named Robot); Caravaggio's and late Rembrandt's shadow pools.
- **Mummy (mummy brown)** — literally ground Egyptian mummies; eaten as medicine before it was painted; Burne-Jones buried his tube in the garden on learning its source (1881); suppliers ran out in the 1960s.
- **Taupe** — French "mole"; notoriously unstandardizable — commercial taupe is browner than any real mole; a caution about how loose color names are.

## Blacks
- **Kohl** — galena (lead sulfide) eye-paint; universal in ancient Egypt; modern analysis found its lead-chloride compounds boosted protective nitric oxide ~240%, reducing eye infections.
- **Payne's gray** — Prussian blue + yellow ochre + crimson lake (formulations vary); a cool blue-black gray named for William Payne; captures atmospheric perspective (distant objects read paler/bluer).
- **Obsidian** — volcanic glass; the Aztec Tezcatlipoca mirror; later Dr. John Dee's spirit-scrying "black stone."
- **Ink** — Egyptian lampblack + gum arabic (~2600 BC, still legible); iron-gall ink from oak galls; "the ink of the scholar is more holy than the blood of the martyr."
- **Charcoal** — restricted-oxygen carbon; Altamira cave art (found 1879 by a 9-year-old, dismissed as forgery for decades); the peppered-moth natural-selection story.
- **Jet** — polished lignite (Whitby); Roman use; Victorian mourning jewelry (Queen Victoria's 40-year mourning); industry collapsed as tastes changed after WWI.
- **Melanin** — eumelanin (brown-black) + pheomelanin (yellow-red); the basis of all human skin/hair/feather color; the raven as the archetypal black animal (Odin's Huginn and Muninn).
- **Pitch black** — clinging tar-darkness; near-universal fear of total darkness (pre-fire evolutionary relic); Latin split *ater* (matte, sinister → "atrocious") from *niger* (glossy, benign); Vantablack traps 99.965% of light.
