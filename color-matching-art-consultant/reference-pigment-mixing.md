## §1. Core Mixing Principles

- **Additive (light)** primaries are red, blue, green; all three combined → **white**; secondaries are *brighter* than primaries. This is the screen/projection world.
- **Subtractive (pigment)** primaries are **red, yellow, blue**; all three combined → a dull near-**black**; secondaries are always **darker and less pure** than the primaries. This is the paint world.
- Pigment mixing absorbs ("subtracts") wavelengths; the more colors added, the more light absorbed, the closer to black/mud.
- **No pigment is a perfect primary** — each leans toward one neighbor (its *bias*).
- Secondaries: red+yellow=orange; yellow+blue=green; blue+red=violet. Tertiaries: a primary + its adjacent secondary (red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet, red-violet).
- **Why mud happens:** when all three primaries end up present in one mix (often via mismatched biases), chroma dies. Complementary pairs each contain all three primaries in aggregate, so mixing complements → neutral darks toward black.

## §2. Pigment Bias / Temperature (named pigments)

Bias is read by brushing the color thinly on white (its undertone shows). Each primary has a warm and a cool version.

**Reds**
- **Cadmium red** — warm, **orange/yellow bias**; opaque, permanent, strong, slow oil dryer.
- **Alizarin crimson** — cool, **blue/violet bias**; transparent, high tinting strength, granulates in watercolour, great for glazing (some permanence concerns).
- **Permanent rose** — cool, blue/violet bias; transparent, very strong; good alizarin substitute.
- **Quinacridone red** — cool, blue bias; transparent, permanent; makes the best purples with ultramarine/cerulean.
- **Vermilion / scarlet lake** — warm, orange bias; intense oranges with yellows.
- **Indian red / Venetian red** — warm earth reds; very opaque, low tinting.

**Yellows**
- **Cadmium yellow** — warm, **red/orange bias**; opaque, permanent.
- **Lemon yellow / cadmium lemon** — cool, **blue/green bias**; makes acidic greens.
- **Aureolin** — cool-neutral, slight green bias; transparent; fine greens with blues.
- **Naples yellow** — warm, subtle; opaque, low tinting; chalky greens with viridian.
- **Yellow ochre / raw sienna** — warm earth yellows; semi-transparent/transparent, low tinting.

**Blues**
- **French ultramarine** — warm, **red/violet bias**; strong, granulates in watercolour, excellent permanence. The standard "warm" blue.
- **Cerulean blue** — cool, **green/yellow bias**; opaque, permanent. The standard "cool" blue.
- **Phthalo blue (monestial)** — cool, green/yellow bias; *extremely* high tinting strength (add tiny amounts), transparent, intense.
- **Cobalt blue** — neutral-cool, slight green bias; excellent grays with raw umber; expensive, permanent.
- **Prussian blue** — cool, strong green bias; very powerful tinting strength.

**Greens** (as convenience colors)
- **Viridian** — cool, blue-green; transparent; the "standard green" base.
- **Phthalo green** — cool, blue bias; even more intense than viridian; use sparingly.
- **Sap green** — warm-neutral, yellow-olive; the most adaptable green, mixes across the palette.
- **Hooker's green** — warm-neutral, yellow-brown; natural-looking; deep greens with reds.
- **Oxide of chromium / terre verte** — muted, gray/olive greens; terre verte is a classic skin underpaint.

**Earths** — raw umber (cool, transparent, great mixer), burnt umber (warm, opaque), raw/burnt sienna (warm, transparent/semi), Vandyke brown (very dense, hard to shift).

**Whites** — titanium (bright, opaque, slow oil dryer), zinc/Chinese white (less opaque, good for glazing), flake/Cremnitz white (lead-based, fast-drying, **toxic**, oils only).

**Blacks** — ivory black (brownish undertone, slow oil dryer), mars/lamp/carbon blacks.

**Grays** — Payne's gray (deep neutral dark, no strong warm/cool bias), Davy's gray (light, transparent).

## §3. Clean vs. Muddy Secondaries (the central rule)

**Clean, intense secondaries** come from mixing the two primaries whose biases lean *toward* the target secondary (no hidden third primary). **Muddy secondaries** come from primaries whose biases lean *away* from each other, dragging in the third primary.

- **Clean violet:** alizarin crimson (blue-biased red) + ultramarine (red-biased blue). Quinacridone red + ultramarine/cobalt also strong. *Dull violet:* cadmium red (orange bias) + cerulean (green bias) → gray-brown.
- **Clean green:** lemon yellow (blue/green bias) + cerulean or phthalo blue (green bias) → bright/acidic. Lemon + viridian also clean. *Natural/quieter green:* cadmium yellow + ultramarine. *Dull/olive:* cadmium yellow (orange bias) + ultramarine (red bias).
- **Clean orange:** cadmium red (orange bias) + cadmium yellow (red/orange bias). *Dull orange:* alizarin crimson (blue bias) + cadmium yellow → brownish.
- Book's own line: "a stronger, more intense violet is made if alizarin crimson (blue bias) is mixed with ultramarine (red bias), than if cadmium red (orange bias) is mixed with cerulean blue (green bias)."
- Viridian + **warm** reds → **brown**; viridian + **cool** reds (alizarin, quinacridone) → fine purples.

## §4. Neutrals, Grays, and Darks

- **Neutrals/grays from complements:** add a touch of a color's complement to subdue ("knock back") its chroma without going to black. E.g., cadmium red + phthalo green → dark gray (then + white for a gray range); permanent rose + mid green → neutral brown-gray.
- **Rich darks without tube black:** ultramarine + burnt umber (warm near-black); alizarin + viridian (deep neutral, classic watercolour "black"); ultramarine + burnt sienna; indigo + burnt sienna; sap green + ivory black (forest dark).
- **Knock-back moves:** dull a red with a touch of green; a blue with a touch of orange; a yellow with a touch of violet.
- **Avoid darkening with black** when possible — black deadens and shifts hue (yellow→olive, red→brown). Prefer a complement, a darker same-hue, or a transparent dark (raw umber, Payne's gray).

## §5. Tints, Shades, Tones

- **Tint** = + white (oil/acrylic/gouache) or + water (watercolour, paper shows through). **Shade** = + black or + complement. **Tone** = relative lightness/darkness; also a color + gray. **Value** = synonym for tone.
- Adding white reduces transparency and can read **chalky** (e.g., cadmium red + titanium white → opaque pink). Transparent colors (alizarin) tint more subtly.
- **Blues + white shift:** ultramarine (red/violet bias) + white drifts toward **lavender/lilac**; cerulean + white stays a cleaner pale blue. Phthalo's tinting strength dominates white mixes — add it in tiny amounts.
- **Watercolour:** lighten with water, not white; use clean water (dirty water muddies everything). Adding white = "body colour" (Chinese white / zinc). Don't mix the water and white methods in one painting.
- **Pastel:** manufacturers supply numbered tint/shade ranges (low = light, mid = purest, high = dark); pressure also lightens/darkens.

## §6. Medium-Specific Behavior

- **Oils:** physical palette mixing; thick (impasto) or thin (glaze, which mixes optically with layers below); slow-drying (ivory black, titanium white slowest; lead whites fast). Transparent pigments glaze, opaque pigments cover. Lead/cadmium/chrome colors have toxicity cautions.
- **Acrylics:** mix like oils but **fast-drying** (great for layered glazes); thin with water for washes; dries slightly **darker** than it looks wet — account for this. Staining colors (phthalo, quinacridone) resist correction.
- **Watercolours:** transparency paramount; lighten with water; overlay transparent washes wet-on-dry (optical mixing). Granulating pigments (ultramarine, manganese/cobalt violet) texture washes; staining pigments (alizarin, phthalo) won't lift once dry.
- **Gouache:** opaque by nature; can go opaque *or* be thinned like watercolour; lighten with water *or* white; can show **watermarks** and dries lighter than it looks wet.
- **Soft pastel:** **cannot mix on a palette** — layer/scumble on paper; optical mixing via strokes; strong pigments overpower light ones.
- **Colored pencil:** essentially **no physical mixing** — build optical mixes in layers; lighten by *less pressure* (let paper show) rather than white over-layering; avoid laying one color too dense before working another over it.
- **Inks:** behave like watercolour; very high tinting strength in blacks.

## §7. Target-Color Recipes

- **Skin (cool, watercolour):** alizarin + cadmium lemon + raw umber base; shadows deepened with Payne's gray + a touch of phthalo/ivory black.
- **Skin (warm, acrylic/oil):** cadmium red + cadmium yellow + burnt umber + ultramarine; lighten with white; shadows = ultramarine added.
- **Foliage greens:** spring/acidic = lemon yellow + viridian/phthalo/cerulean; natural mid = cadmium yellow + viridian/Hooker's/sap; olive = cad yellow deep + terre verte / oxide of chromium / Prussian blue; forest = cadmium red + Hooker's green, or sap green + ivory black; sap green is the most adaptable.
- **Skies:** summer = light cerulean wash; deep = phthalo washes; winter/cool = phthalo blue + Payne's gray + raw umber; snow shadow = stronger phthalo blue.
- **Shadows:** cool = ultramarine/phthalo + a touch of warm or Payne's gray; neutral = ultramarine + raw umber; warm dark = ultramarine + burnt umber.
- **Browns/earths:** ochre = cad yellow light + raw umber; burnt-umber substitute = raw umber + alizarin + a little ultramarine; chestnut = cad yellow + Venetian red; sepia-ish = alizarin + burnt umber.
- **Grays:** neutral = cad red + phthalo green (+ white/water); cool = cerulean or ultramarine + raw umber; warm = raw umber + ultramarine + white; cobalt + raw umber gives excellent grays.

## §8. Common Problems & Fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Muddy secondary | Two primaries' biases bring in the third primary | Use primaries whose biases lean *toward* the target secondary |
| Violet goes gray | Cadmium red (orange) + cerulean (green) | Switch to alizarin + ultramarine |
| Green goes olive | Warm yellow + warm blue | Use a cool yellow (lemon) + a green-biased blue |
| Orange goes brown | Cool red (alizarin) in the mix | Use cadmium red + cadmium yellow |
| Color deadened | Black added to darken | Darken with complement, darker same-hue, or Payne's gray |
| One pigment dominates | Phthalo/Prussian/dioxazine very high tinting strength | Add them in tiny amounts to the weaker color |
| Chalky tint | Too much opaque white | Use transparent (zinc) white; reduce white; keep a transparent component |
| Washes muddy (watercolour) | Dirty mixing water | Change to clean water |
| Unwanted granulation | Ultramarine/manganese/cobalt violet | Use cobalt or cerulean instead |
| Can't correct (watercolour) | Staining pigment dried in | Plan staining colors carefully; use non-staining where corrections likely |
